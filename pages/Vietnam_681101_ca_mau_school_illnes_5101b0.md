## Introduction

In December 2001, a primary school in Ca Mau City, the southernmost city in [Vietnam]({{ 'when-belief-fear-and-crisis-swept/' | relative_url }}), became the scene of one of the country's best documented episodes of mass psychogenic illness, sometimes called mass sociogenic illness. After receiving an oral cholera vaccine during a public health campaign, dozens of children rapidly developed genuine symptoms including trembling, headaches, nausea and cold extremities. Rumours of poisoning spread quickly, raising fears about the safety of the vaccination programme. However, repeated investigations found no evidence of contaminated vaccine, poisoning or infectious disease. Instead, investigators concluded that fear itself had spread through a closely connected group of pupils, producing real physical symptoms without an underlying toxic or infectious cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0264410X03004985" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mass psychogenic illness following oral cholera immunization in Ca Mau City, Vietnam - ScienceDirectNovember 7, 2003...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2003</span></span></span>

The Ca Mau incident remains an important case in Vietnam because it demonstrates how anxiety, social observation and rumours can generate a convincing outbreak of illness while leaving no biological cause to discover. It is also frequently cited internationally in research on vaccine confidence and the management of anxiety-related events during mass immunisation campaigns.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0264410X03004985" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mass psychogenic illness following oral cholera immunization in Ca Mau City, Vietnam - ScienceDirectNovember 7, 2003...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2003</span></span></span>

## What happened after the cholera vaccination?

The incident occurred on 18 December 2001 during an emergency cholera vaccination campaign. Three laboratory-confirmed cholera cases had recently been detected elsewhere in Ca Mau Province, prompting health authorities to vaccinate schoolchildren living in areas considered vulnerable because of nearby waterways. The vaccine used was Vietnam's domestically produced killed oral cholera vaccine, which had already been used extensively in public health campaigns.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0264410X03004985" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mass psychogenic illness following oral cholera immunization in Ca Mau City, Vietnam - ScienceDirectNovember 7, 2003...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2003</span></span></span>

At Primary School No. 1 in Commune 8, 234 pupils received the oral vaccine during the morning. Shortly afterwards, three boys in one classroom complained of trembling, headache and nausea. Teachers escorted them across the school grounds to the library, where other pupils could see them being removed. As concern spread, increasing numbers of children began reporting similar symptoms. Parents, alerted by the commotion, rushed to the school, adding to the atmosphere of alarm.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0264410X03004985" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mass psychogenic illness following oral cholera immunization in Ca Mau City, Vietnam - ScienceDirectNovember 7, 2003...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2003</span></span></span>

By the end of the episode:

* The most commonly reported symptom was cold hands and feet, followed by headache, nausea and trembling.
* All of the affected children recovered within a few hours.
* No long-term health effects or repeat episodes were reported among those involved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0264410X03004985" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mass psychogenic illness following oral cholera immunization in Ca Mau City, Vietnam - ScienceDirectNovember 7, 2003...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2003</span></span></span>

## Why investigators ruled out poisoning and infection

Because newspapers initially described the incident as possible poisoning, Vietnamese health authorities conducted detailed investigations. The first inquiry involved provincial and national public health agencies, while a later investigation included specialists from the International Vaccine Institute after international concern grew over media reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0264410X03004985" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mass psychogenic illness following oral cholera immunization in Ca Mau City, Vietnam - ScienceDirectNovember 7, 2003...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2003</span></span></span>

Investigators examined multiple possible explanations.

First, they inspected vaccine transport, refrigeration, storage and administration procedures. No significant problems were identified, and laboratory testing of vaccine samples from the same production batch found no contamination or abnormality.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0264410X03004985" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mass psychogenic illness following oral cholera immunization in Ca Mau City, Vietnam - ScienceDirectNovember 7, 2003...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2003</span></span></span>

Second, clinicians reviewed the children's medical findings. Although the symptoms were genuine and distressing, they did not fit the pattern expected from poisoning or infectious disease. The illness appeared extremely quickly, spread rapidly through observation of other affected pupils, produced no objective evidence of toxic exposure, and resolved within hours without lasting harm.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0264410X03004985" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mass psychogenic illness following oral cholera immunization in Ca Mau City, Vietnam - ScienceDirectNovember 7, 2003...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2003</span></span></span>

Third, investigators considered the pattern of spread. The first affected pupils were visible to classmates as they were escorted away, and new cases appeared after children witnessed or heard about earlier illnesses. Such social transmission is characteristic of mass psychogenic illness rather than exposure to a poisonous substance, which would normally affect people according to dose rather than social contact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0264410X03004985" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mass psychogenic illness following oral cholera immunization in Ca Mau City, Vietnam - ScienceDirectNovember 7, 2003...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2003</span></span></span>

After reviewing all available evidence, investigators concluded that the outbreak was best explained as mass psychogenic illness rather than vaccine toxicity or infection. Public health authorities subsequently administered the second vaccine dose with the agreement of parents' representatives, and the wider vaccination campaign continued.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0264410X03004985" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mass psychogenic illness following oral cholera immunization in Ca Mau City, Vietnam - ScienceDirectNovember 7, 2003...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2003</span></span></span>

## How mass psychogenic illness spread through the school

Mass psychogenic illness does not mean that people are pretending to be ill. The symptoms are real, but they arise through psychological and social mechanisms rather than poisoning or infection. The Ca Mau investigation illustrates several features recognised in similar outbreaks around the world.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0264410X03004985" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mass psychogenic illness following oral cholera immunization in Ca Mau City, Vietnam - ScienceDirectNovember 7, 2003...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2003</span></span></span>

Several conditions helped the illness spread.

**A stressful situation.** The vaccination campaign was organised at short notice because of recent cholera cases. According to investigators, parents were not informed in advance, leaving little opportunity to explain what children should expect.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0264410X03004985" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mass psychogenic illness following oral cholera immunization in Ca Mau City, Vietnam - ScienceDirectNovember 7, 2003...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2003</span></span></span>

**Visible illness.** The first pupils became unwell in front of classmates and were escorted through the school grounds. Seeing apparently sick children heightened anxiety among others who had received the same vaccine.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0264410X03004985" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mass psychogenic illness following oral cholera immunization in Ca Mau City, Vietnam - ScienceDirectNovember 7, 2003...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2003</span></span></span>

**Rapid rumours.** As parents gathered and concern spread, expectations that something dangerous had happened reinforced the cycle of anxiety. Media reports referring to possible poisoning amplified those fears beyond the school itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0264410X03004985" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mass psychogenic illness following oral cholera immunization in Ca Mau City, Vietnam - ScienceDirectNovember 7, 2003...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2003</span></span></span>

**Benign clinical course.** The symptoms were dramatic enough to prompt hospital visits but disappeared quickly without evidence of poisoning or permanent injury, matching the typical pattern seen in documented psychogenic outbreaks.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0264410X03004985" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mass psychogenic illness following oral cholera immunization in Ca Mau City, Vietnam - ScienceDirectNovember 7, 2003...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2003</span></span></span>

One unusual feature attracted scientific attention. Many published school outbreaks have involved a strong predominance of girls, yet the Ca Mau episode affected boys and girls in almost equal numbers. Researchers highlighted this as an uncommon characteristic that broadened understanding of how such episodes can occur.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0264410X03004985" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mass psychogenic illness following oral cholera immunization in Ca Mau City, Vietnam - ScienceDirectNovember 7, 2003...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2003</span></span></span>

## Why the case remains important

The Ca Mau outbreak has become one of the most frequently cited Vietnamese examples in international literature on immunisation stress-related responses and anxiety-related adverse events following vaccination. Rather than demonstrating a dangerous vaccine, it illustrates how fear can disrupt vaccination campaigns even when the vaccine itself is safe.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4151976/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCOral Cholera Vaccine Development and Use in Vietnam</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PMCOral Cholera Vaccine Development and Use in Vietnam</span></span></span>

Researchers have drawn several practical lessons from the case:

* explain vaccination campaigns clearly to schools, families and children beforehand;
* train health workers to recognise anxiety-related clusters quickly;
* avoid unnecessary emergency medical interventions once toxic causes have been excluded;
* communicate carefully with the media to prevent rumours from overwhelming verified evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0264410X03004985" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mass psychogenic illness following oral cholera immunization in Ca Mau City, Vietnam - ScienceDirectNovember 7, 2003...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2003</span></span></span>

## What the Ca Mau illness tells us about contagious fear

The Ca Mau school outbreak occupies a distinctive place in Vietnam's history of collective fear because it was investigated thoroughly and explained without resorting to myths, supernatural beliefs or conspiracy theories. The children's symptoms were genuine, but the evidence showed that they spread through observation, expectation and shared anxiety rather than exposure to poison or disease.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0264410X03004985" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mass psychogenic illness following oral cholera immunization in Ca Mau City, Vietnam - ScienceDirectNovember 7, 2003...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2003</span></span></span>

For historians, psychologists and public health specialists, the episode demonstrates that contagious fear can produce remarkably convincing physical illness in tightly connected communities. It also shows why rapid, transparent investigation matters: without careful evidence gathering, temporary episodes of mass psychogenic illness can easily become lasting stories of poisoning, cover-ups or unsafe vaccines, even when the available evidence points elsewhere.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0264410X03004985" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mass psychogenic illness following oral cholera immunization in Ca Mau City, Vietnam - ScienceDirectNovember 7, 2003...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2003</span></span></span>
