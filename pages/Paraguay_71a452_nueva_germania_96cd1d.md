## Introduction

Nueva Germania was one of the most ambitious and revealing ideological settlement projects in [Paraguay]({{ 'when-belief-reshaped-life-in-paraguay/' | relative_url }})'s history. Founded in the late 1880s by the German antisemite Bernhard Förster and his wife Elisabeth Förster-Nietzsche, sister of the philosopher Friedrich Nietzsche, it was intended to prove that a racially "pure" German community could flourish far from what its founders regarded as the corrupting influences of modern Europe. Rather than becoming a model society, however, the colony was overwhelmed by isolation, poor planning, disease, financial collapse and internal conflict. Within only a few years, its utopian claims had unravelled.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://univerlag.uni-goettingen.de/handle/3/isbn-978-3-86395-636-3?locale-attribute=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: univerlag.uni-goettingen.de">[uni-goettingen.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">univerlag.uni-goettingen.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">People and Identities in Nueva Germania</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">People and Identities in Nueva Germania</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Paraguay_71a452_nueva_germania_96cd1d-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Nueva Germania illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Paraguay_71a452_nueva_germania_96cd1d-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Paraguay_71a452_nueva_germania_96cd1d-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

Nueva Germania is relevant to the history of collective belief because it shows how an ideological vision can take on the character of a social experiment. Although it was not a religious movement or a classic "cult", it rested on a shared conviction that an imagined community could remake human society through racial separation. The gulf between that belief and the realities of life in rural Paraguay makes the colony an important case study in the failure of ideological utopianism.

## The founders and their racial vision

Bernhard Förster emerged from the increasingly nationalist and antisemitic politics of late nineteenth-century Germany. Convinced that German society was supposedly being weakened by Jewish influence, he argued that the solution was not political reform but physical separation. Together with Elisabeth Förster-Nietzsche, he promoted the creation of a new settlement where only carefully selected German colonists would establish an ideal society based on what they considered "Aryan" values.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://english.elpais.com/international/2023-05-09/nueva-germania-the-aryan-utopia-of-nietzsches-sister-and-bernhard-forster-in-paraguay.html?outputType=amp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: english.elpais.com">[EL PAÍS English]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">english.elpais.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on elpais.com.</span></span></span>

Paraguay appeared attractive for several reasons. The country was encouraging European immigration after the catastrophic War of the Triple Alliance, which had devastated its population and economy. The government hoped foreign settlers would stimulate agricultural development, while Förster saw an opportunity to establish an isolated colony beyond European political constraints. The project therefore combined Paraguayan nation-building with a very different ideological agenda brought by the settlers themselves.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://amp.dw.com/en/nueva-germania-the-failed-aryan-project-in-paraguay/a-76817647" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: amp.dw.com">[DW News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">amp.dw.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">News Nueva Germania: The failed &#x27;Aryan project&#x27; in Paraguay</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DW NewsNueva Germania: The failed &#x27;Aryan project&#x27; in ParaguayApril 18, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 18, 2026</span></span></span>

Promotional literature described Nueva Germania as a healthy agricultural paradise where disciplined German families could preserve their language, culture and ancestry. These promises greatly understated the environmental challenges awaiting settlers and exaggerated the organisers' financial and logistical capacity. Later research has shown that recruitment relied as much on utopian rhetoric as on realistic planning.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uni-tuebingen.de/fakultaeten/philosophische-fakultaet/fachbereiche/altertums-und-kunstwissenschaften/ur-und-fruehgeschichte-und-archaeologie-des-mittelalters/abteilungen/mittelalter/forschungsprojekte/laufende-projekte/die-kolonie-nueva-germania-paraguay-und-ihre-ressourcen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uni-tuebingen.de">[Universität Tübingen]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uni-tuebingen.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uni-tuebingen.de.</span></span></span>

## Isolation, disease and practical failure

The colony's greatest enemy was not ethnic diversity but reality. The settlement lay in an isolated region with poor transport links, limited infrastructure and demanding agricultural conditions. Many newcomers had little experience of tropical farming and found themselves struggling to clear dense vegetation, cultivate unfamiliar soils and cope with heat, insects and disease.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uni-tuebingen.de/fakultaeten/philosophische-fakultaet/fachbereiche/altertums-und-kunstwissenschaften/ur-und-fruehgeschichte-und-archaeologie-des-mittelalters/abteilungen/mittelalter/forschungsprojekte/laufende-projekte/die-kolonie-nueva-germania-paraguay-und-ihre-ressourcen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uni-tuebingen.de">[Universität Tübingen]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uni-tuebingen.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uni-tuebingen.de.</span></span></span>

Illness, food shortages and financial problems quickly undermined morale. The founders failed to attract as many settlers and investors as they had promised, leaving the community short of labour and capital. Rather than demonstrating the superiority of their social model, the colony became increasingly dependent on practical cooperation with neighbouring Paraguayans and local Guaraní people, whose knowledge of the landscape proved essential to survival. Historians note that this dependence fundamentally contradicted the founders' claims of self-sufficient racial isolation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://amp.dw.com/en/nueva-germania-the-failed-aryan-project-in-paraguay/a-76817647" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: amp.dw.com">[dw.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">amp.dw.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">News Nueva Germania: The failed &#x27;Aryan project&#x27; in Paraguay</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DW NewsNueva Germania: The failed &#x27;Aryan project&#x27; in ParaguayApril 18, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 18, 2026</span></span></span>

Internal tensions also emerged. Contemporary accounts describe disagreements over leadership, finances and daily living conditions. Some colonists abandoned ideals such as vegetarianism when survival became more pressing than ideology. Bernhard Förster accumulated heavy debts, became increasingly despondent and died in 1889, widely believed to have taken his own life, although some details remain uncertain in the historical record. Elisabeth attempted to keep the project alive before eventually returning to Germany, leaving the remaining settlers to adapt without the original leadership.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernhard_F%C3%B6rster" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bernhard Förster</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bernhard Förster</span></span></span>

The rapid collapse illustrates an important feature of ideological communities more generally: highly ambitious social visions often depend on practical assumptions about economics, health and leadership that prove far more difficult than their founders anticipate.

## Why the racial experiment failed

Nueva Germania did not collapse because outsiders destroyed it. Instead, the colony failed through the interaction of ideology with ordinary human realities.

Several factors proved decisive:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Environmental hardship**, including difficult farming conditions and tropical diseases unfamiliar to many settlers.
* **Geographical isolation**, which made transport, trade and communication expensive and unreliable.
* **Financial weakness**, as promised investment and immigration never reached the levels needed to sustain the settlement.
* **Leadership failures**, including unrealistic planning and growing personal debts.
* **Human adaptation**, as surviving colonists increasingly relied on relationships with local Paraguayan society rather than maintaining strict racial separation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uni-tuebingen.de/fakultaeten/philosophische-fakultaet/fachbereiche/altertums-und-kunstwissenschaften/ur-und-fruehgeschichte-und-archaeologie-des-mittelalters/abteilungen/mittelalter/forschungsprojekte/laufende-projekte/die-kolonie-nueva-germania-paraguay-und-ihre-ressourcen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uni-tuebingen.de">[uni-tuebingen.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uni-tuebingen.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uni-tuebingen.de.</span></span></span>

</div>

For historians, these practical failures matter because they expose the gap between ideological certainty and lived experience. The colony's racial programme could not be maintained once ordinary needs—food, labour, marriage, trade and health—took priority over abstract theories.

## Myth, memory and mixed identities

The history of Nueva Germania has often been retold in highly dramatic ways because of Elisabeth Förster-Nietzsche's later role in promoting aspects of her brother Friedrich Nietzsche's legacy and because the colony anticipated themes that later appeared in twentieth-century racial ideologies. Yet modern scholars caution against treating today's community simply as a surviving "Nazi village" or as a living museum of nineteenth-century racism.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://univerlag.uni-goettingen.de/handle/3/isbn-978-3-86395-636-3?locale-attribute=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: univerlag.uni-goettingen.de">[univerlag.uni-goettingen.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">univerlag.uni-goettingen.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">People and Identities in Nueva Germania</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">People and Identities in Nueva Germania</span></span></span>

Ethnographic research by Jonatan Kurzwelly shows that contemporary residents possess complex and varied identities. Many families descend from both German settlers and Paraguayan or Guaraní ancestors. Local identity has developed through generations of cultural mixing, economic change and adaptation rather than through preservation of the founders' original ideology. Residents frequently reject sensational portrayals that reduce their community to its origins alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://univerlag.uni-goettingen.de/handle/3/isbn-978-3-86395-636-3?locale-attribute=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: univerlag.uni-goettingen.de">[univerlag.uni-goettingen.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">univerlag.uni-goettingen.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">People and Identities in Nueva Germania</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">People and Identities in Nueva Germania</span></span></span>

This distinction is important. While the colony was undeniably founded upon racist and antisemitic principles, the lives of present-day inhabitants cannot simply be read as a continuation of those beliefs.

## Why Nueva Germania matters in Paraguay's history of collective belief

Nueva Germania occupies an unusual place within Paraguay's wider history of collective beliefs and social movements. Unlike prophetic Indigenous movements or later moral panics surrounding unfamiliar religious groups, it represented an imported ideological experiment built around racial nationalism rather than religious revelation.

Its significance lies in demonstrating how a powerful shared belief can organise migration, reshape lives and inspire remarkable personal sacrifice even when its assumptions are detached from social and environmental reality. The settlers were persuaded that they could create a fundamentally new society by controlling ancestry and isolation. Instead, survival depended upon cooperation, adaptation and integration with the surrounding Paraguayan world.

For that reason, Nueva Germania is remembered less as a successful colony than as a cautionary example of how utopian projects built on exclusionary ideology can collapse when confronted by the practical demands of everyday life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://univerlag.uni-goettingen.de/handle/3/isbn-978-3-86395-636-3?locale-attribute=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: univerlag.uni-goettingen.de">[uni-goettingen.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">univerlag.uni-goettingen.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">People and Identities in Nueva Germania</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">People and Identities in Nueva Germania</span></span></span>
