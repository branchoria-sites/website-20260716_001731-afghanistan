## Introduction

On the evening of 12 February 1949, one of [Ecuador]({{ 'when-fear-became-reality-in-ecuador/' | relative_url }})’s most trusted radio stations convinced thousands of listeners that Martians were invading the country. What began as an adaptation of H. G. Wells’s *The War of the Worlds* rapidly became one of the deadliest media panics in history. The broadcast, transmitted by Radio Quito in the style of a genuine news bulletin, prompted frightened residents to flee, telephone relatives and search for information. When the station finally announced that the invasion had been fictional, fear turned into fury. An angry crowd attacked the building that housed both Radio Quito and the newspaper *El Comercio*, setting it on fire and killing several people inside. The episode has remained one of Ecuador's best-known examples of how trusted media, public anxiety and misinformation can combine to produce catastrophic consequences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://radioambulante.org/en/translation/the-extraterrestrials-repeat-translation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: radioambulante.org">[Radio Ambulante]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">radioambulante.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Radio Ambulante The Extraterrestrials [Repeat] &#124; Translation &#124; Radio Ambulante</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Radio AmbulanteThe Extraterrestrials [Repeat] &#124; Translation &#124; Radio AmbulanteDecember 31, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2024</span></span></span>

## Why listeners believed Radio Quito

By 1949, radio had become Ecuador’s fastest and most influential source of news. Radio Quito enjoyed an especially strong reputation because it belonged to the same company as the respected daily newspaper *El Comercio*. For many families, hearing an announcement from Radio Quito carried the same authority as reading a headline in the morning paper.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://radioambulante.org/en/translation/the-extraterrestrials-repeat-translation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: radioambulante.org">[Radio Ambulante]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">radioambulante.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Radio Ambulante The Extraterrestrials [Repeat] &#124; Translation &#124; Radio Ambulante</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Radio AmbulanteThe Extraterrestrials [Repeat] &#124; Translation &#124; Radio AmbulanteDecember 31, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2024</span></span></span>

The programme was carefully designed to exploit that trust. Rather than presenting itself as a drama, it interrupted an evening music programme with what sounded like breaking news. Broadcasters reported that a mysterious object had landed near the city of Latacunga before describing hostile spacecraft advancing towards Quito. Actors played reporters and officials, while fake live updates and realistic sound effects created the impression that listeners were hearing an unfolding national emergency rather than fiction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://radioambulante.org/en/translation/the-extraterrestrials-translation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: radioambulante.org">[Radio Ambulante]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">radioambulante.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Radio Ambulante The Extraterrestrials</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Radio AmbulanteThe Extraterrestrials - Translation - Radio Ambulante...</span></span></span>

The broadcast also drew on familiar geography. Instead of setting the story in England, as in Wells's novel, or New Jersey, as in Orson Welles's famous 1938 American adaptation, the writers placed the invasion in recognisable Ecuadorian locations. That local realism made the story far more convincing to people with no reason to expect a theatrical performance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://radioambulante.org/en/translation/the-extraterrestrials-translation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: radioambulante.org">[Radio Ambulante]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">radioambulante.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Radio Ambulante The Extraterrestrials</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Radio AmbulanteThe Extraterrestrials - Translation - Radio Ambulante...</span></span></span>

## How the fake invasion broadcast worked

The production was created by artistic director Leonardo Páez and writer Eduardo Alcaraz. Like Orson Welles's earlier adaptation, it blurred the line between entertainment and live reporting. News flashes interrupted music, correspondents described explosions and destruction, and announcers spoke with the calm authority expected during a genuine emergency.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://radioambulante.org/en/translation/the-extraterrestrials-translation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: radioambulante.org">[Radio Ambulante]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">radioambulante.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Radio Ambulante The Extraterrestrials</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Radio AmbulanteThe Extraterrestrials - Translation - Radio Ambulante...</span></span></span>

Several factors made the illusion unusually effective:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* **Authentic presentation.** The programme copied the style and pacing of emergency news broadcasts.
* **Recognisable locations.** Reports mentioned Latacunga and the road towards Quito rather than fictional places.
* **Limited ways to verify information.** Few households had immediate access to alternative news sources or telephones capable of confirming events independently.
* **High public confidence in radio.** Many listeners regarded radio as the quickest and most reliable source of urgent information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://radioambulante.org/en/translation/the-extraterrestrials-repeat-translation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: radioambulante.org">[Radio Ambulante]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">radioambulante.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Radio Ambulante The Extraterrestrials [Repeat] &#124; Translation &#124; Radio Ambulante</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Radio AmbulanteThe Extraterrestrials [Repeat] &#124; Translation &#124; Radio AmbulanteDecember 31, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2024</span></span></span>

Some listeners reportedly rushed to churches, while others tried to contact relatives or prepared to escape the city. Although later retellings have sometimes exaggerated the scale of the panic, contemporary accounts agree that enough people accepted the story as genuine to create widespread alarm across Quito.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://radioambulante.org/en/translation/the-extraterrestrials-repeat-translation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: radioambulante.org">[Radio Ambulante]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">radioambulante.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Radio Ambulante The Extraterrestrials [Repeat] &#124; Translation &#124; Radio Ambulante</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Radio AmbulanteThe Extraterrestrials [Repeat] &#124; Translation &#124; Radio AmbulanteDecember 31, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2024</span></span></span>

## From public panic to a deadly riot

The turning point came when station staff realised that listeners had taken the story literally. They interrupted the programme to announce that there was no invasion and that the broadcast had been a fictional adaptation. Rather than calming the audience, the announcement transformed fear into outrage. Many people believed they had been cruelly deceived after experiencing genuine terror.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://radioambulante.org/en/translation/the-extraterrestrials-repeat-translation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: radioambulante.org">[Radio Ambulante]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">radioambulante.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Radio Ambulante The Extraterrestrials [Repeat] &#124; Translation &#124; Radio Ambulante</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Radio AmbulanteThe Extraterrestrials [Repeat] &#124; Translation &#124; Radio AmbulanteDecember 31, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2024</span></span></span>

Crowds gathered outside the building shared by Radio Quito and *El Comercio*. Protesters threw stones, forced their way inside and eventually set the premises ablaze. The violence overwhelmed attempts to restore order, and firefighters initially struggled to reach the scene because parts of the crowd obstructed rescue efforts. Police and military units were eventually deployed before the fire could be controlled.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://radioambulante.org/en/translation/the-extraterrestrials-repeat-translation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: radioambulante.org">[Radio Ambulante]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">radioambulante.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Radio Ambulante The Extraterrestrials [Repeat] &#124; Translation &#124; Radio Ambulante</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Radio AmbulanteThe Extraterrestrials [Repeat] &#124; Translation &#124; Radio AmbulanteDecember 31, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2024</span></span></span>

The building was almost completely destroyed. Modern historical research generally concludes that at least seven or eight people died in the fire, including members of the broadcasting team and relatives who happened to be inside. Later retellings sometimes claim far higher death tolls—occasionally as many as twenty—but historians note that these larger figures often combine confirmed fatalities with unverified reports of later deaths or suicides. The exact number therefore remains disputed, although the riot unquestionably became one of the deadliest consequences of any fictional broadcast.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://radioambulante.org/en/translation/the-extraterrestrials-repeat-translation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: radioambulante.org">[Radio Ambulante]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">radioambulante.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Radio Ambulante The Extraterrestrials [Repeat] &#124; Translation &#124; Radio Ambulante</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Radio AmbulanteThe Extraterrestrials [Repeat] &#124; Translation &#124; Radio AmbulanteDecember 31, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2024</span></span></span>

One of the episode's most tragic ironies was that desperate appeals broadcast from inside the burning station were reportedly dismissed by some listeners as simply another part of the drama, delaying assistance at a critical moment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://radioambulante.org/en/translation/the-extraterrestrials-repeat-translation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: radioambulante.org">[Radio Ambulante]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">radioambulante.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Radio Ambulante The Extraterrestrials [Repeat] &#124; Translation &#124; Radio Ambulante</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Radio AmbulanteThe Extraterrestrials [Repeat] &#124; Translation &#124; Radio AmbulanteDecember 31, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2024</span></span></span>

## Why Ecuador was vulnerable to this kind of panic

The incident cannot be explained simply by claiming that listeners were unusually gullible. Historians instead point to a combination of social and historical circumstances.

The memory of the 1941 war with Peru remained fresh, while the Second World War had ended only a few years earlier. News of devastating new weapons, aerial attacks and global conflict had made extraordinary disasters seem more imaginable than they had before the war. Radio itself was still a comparatively new mass medium whose authority was rarely questioned.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://radioambulante.org/en/translation/the-extraterrestrials-repeat-translation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: radioambulante.org">[Radio Ambulante]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">radioambulante.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Radio Ambulante The Extraterrestrials [Repeat] &#124; Translation &#124; Radio Ambulante</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Radio AmbulanteThe Extraterrestrials [Repeat] &#124; Translation &#124; Radio AmbulanteDecember 31, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2024</span></span></span>

Equally important was the structure of information. In 1949 there was no internet, television or rapid fact-checking. Once trusted broadcasters announced an emergency, many listeners had no practical way to verify whether it was real before reacting emotionally. The speed with which fear spread reflected the communications environment as much as the content of the programme itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://radioambulante.org/en/translation/the-extraterrestrials-repeat-translation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: radioambulante.org">[Radio Ambulante]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">radioambulante.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Radio Ambulante The Extraterrestrials [Repeat] &#124; Translation &#124; Radio Ambulante</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Radio AmbulanteThe Extraterrestrials [Repeat] &#124; Translation &#124; Radio AmbulanteDecember 31, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2024</span></span></span>

## What happened afterwards

The fire destroyed Radio Quito's facilities, including the original script and recording of the broadcast. As a result, no original audio survives, and modern recreations are based on surviving documents, witness testimony and later reconstructions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://radioambulante.org/en/translation/the-extraterrestrials-repeat-translation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: radioambulante.org">[Radio Ambulante]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">radioambulante.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Radio Ambulante The Extraterrestrials [Repeat] &#124; Translation &#124; Radio Ambulante</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Radio AmbulanteThe Extraterrestrials [Repeat] &#124; Translation &#124; Radio AmbulanteDecember 31, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2024</span></span></span>

Leonardo Páez became one of the principal public figures associated with the disaster. Facing intense criticism and fearing retaliation, he eventually left Ecuador and spent much of the rest of his life in Venezuela. Radio Quito itself remained off the air for approximately two years while its facilities were rebuilt.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_War_of_the_Worlds_%281938_radio_drama%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The War of the Worlds (1938 radio drama</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The War of the Worlds (1938 radio drama</span></span></span>

The tragedy also became a cautionary tale within broadcasting. Although radio dramas continued to flourish throughout Latin America, producers became more conscious of clearly distinguishing fiction from genuine news, especially when using documentary-style techniques.

## Myth, memory and historical interpretation

The Radio Quito disaster is often compared with Orson Welles's 1938 American broadcast of *The War of the Worlds*. That comparison is useful but also misleading.

Modern scholarship suggests that the panic caused by the American broadcast was substantially exaggerated by newspapers competing with radio for audiences. By contrast, the Ecuadorian case involved well-documented violence, destruction of property and multiple deaths. While stories about the scale of the initial panic have grown over time, there is no serious historical dispute that the riot itself occurred or that it had fatal consequences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_War_of_the_Worlds_%281938_radio_drama%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The War of the Worlds (1938 radio drama</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The War of the Worlds (1938 radio drama</span></span></span>

Today the event is remembered less as evidence of irrational public behaviour than as a powerful demonstration of how credibility, emotion and media authority interact. Trusted institutions can persuade audiences of extraordinary claims when those claims are delivered in familiar formats during periods of uncertainty. The tragedy therefore occupies an important place not only in Ecuador's social history but also in the wider history of misinformation, public trust and mass communication.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://radioambulante.org/en/audio-en/the-extraterrestrials-repeat" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: radioambulante.org">[Radio Ambulante]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">radioambulante.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Radio Ambulante The Extraterrestrials [Repeat] &#124; Radio Ambulante</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Radio Ambulante The Extraterrestrials [Repeat] &#124; Radio Ambulante</span></span></span>
