## Introduction

The Corfu blood libel of 1891 was not an outbreak of unexplained “mass hysteria”, but a case of organised persecution driven by a long-standing antisemitic conspiracy theory. After the body of an eight-year-old Jewish girl, Rubina Sardas, was discovered in Corfu, false rumours claimed that she was really a Christian child murdered by Jews for ritual purposes. Those allegations, rooted in the medieval blood libel myth, spread rapidly through parts of the local press, sections of the police, political figures and the wider public. The result was weeks of anti-Jewish violence, deaths, destruction of property and the displacement of a large part of Corfu's Jewish community. Historians now treat the episode as one of the most significant antisemitic outbreaks in modern Greek history and as a powerful example of how conspiracy myths can transform prejudice into collective violence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ejournals.epublishing.ekt.gr/index.php/historicalReview/article/view/3994" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ejournals.epublishing.ekt.gr">[ekt.gr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ejournals.epublishing.ekt.gr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Anti-Semitic Disturbances on Corfu and Zakynthos in 1891 and their Socio-political Consequences&#124;The Historical Review/La Rev...</span></span></span>

## The murder of Rubina Sardas and the false accusation

The events began in April 1891 when Rubina Sardas, a young Jewish girl from Corfu, disappeared. After a search, her body was found hidden inside a sack. She had suffered severe injuries, and the circumstances immediately provoked public speculation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://blog.nli.org.il/en/corfu_jews/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blog.nli.org.il">[הספרנים]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blog.nli.org.il</span><span class="citation-popover-title">הספרניםThe Lost History of the Jews of Corfu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">June 5, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 5, 2024</span></span></span>

Instead of treating the crime as the murder of a Jewish child, rumours quickly transformed its meaning. A story spread that the victim was actually a Christian orphan called Maria Desylla who had supposedly been raised in a Jewish household before being sacrificed for Passover rituals. This accusation echoed the centuries-old blood libel, the false belief that Jews kidnapped and murdered Christian children to use their blood in religious ceremonies. The allegation had no basis in Jewish belief or practice, but it had repeatedly been used across Europe to justify persecution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/blood-libel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jewishvirtuallibrary.org">[Jewish Virtual Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jewishvirtuallibrary.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Jewish Virtual Library Blood Libel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Jewish Virtual Library Blood Libel</span></span></span>

Subsequent investigations steadily dismantled the rumour. Witnesses confirmed that the dead child was Rubina Sardas, born to Jewish parents in Corfu. Religious records, school testimony and official inquiries all contradicted claims that she was a Christian child. Medical experts also concluded that many injuries had been inflicted after death, probably to disguise the real circumstances of the murder rather than as evidence of any ritual killing. Despite these findings, the conspiracy theory had already gained enough momentum to fuel violence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.toviapreschel.com/the-corfu-blood-libel-of-1891/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: toviapreschel.com">[Tovia Preschel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">toviapreschel.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the corfu blood libel of 1891</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tovia PreschelThe Corfu Blood Libel of 1891May 25, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 25, 2023</span></span></span>

## How politics, newspapers and Easter tensions spread the rumour

The rapid spread of the accusation cannot be explained simply by grief or confusion. Historians argue that it succeeded because it fitted older prejudices that already existed within sections of Corfu's Christian population. Blood libel stories had circulated elsewhere in Europe for centuries, and Orthodox Easter, which fell close to Jewish Passover in 1891, was traditionally a period when anti-Jewish tensions could become more visible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ejournals.epublishing.ekt.gr/index.php/historicalReview/article/view/3994" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ejournals.epublishing.ekt.gr">[eJournals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ejournals.epublishing.ekt.gr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Anti-Semitic Disturbances on Corfu and Zakynthos in 1891 and their Socio-political Consequences&#124;The Historical Review/La Rev...</span></span></span>

The circulation of rumours was reinforced by several interconnected forces.

* **Sensational reporting:** Local newspapers published dramatic and often unverified claims, helping transform speculation into apparent fact.
* **Failures by officials:** Research based on archival records suggests that some local police officers helped circulate ritual-murder rumours instead of suppressing them, although judicial authorities were generally more cautious and increasingly rejected the allegations.
* **Political interests:** Local rivalries and wider debates over the position of Jews in Greek society encouraged some politicians and public figures to exploit the accusations rather than calm public opinion.
* **Existing economic and social tensions:** Corfu's Jewish community had experienced greater civil equality after the Ionian Islands joined [Greece]({{ 'when-fear-and-belief-gripped-greece/' | relative_url }}). Historians argue that economic competition and resentment towards successful Jewish merchants created fertile ground for antisemitic narratives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.quest-cdecjournal.it/antisemitic-rumours-and-violence-in-corfu-at-the-end-of-19th-century/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: quest-cdecjournal.it">[quest-cdecjournal.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">quest-cdecjournal.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Quest CDEC JournalAntisemitic Rumours and Violence in Corfu at the End of 19th Century - Quest. Issues in Contemporary Jewish History...</span></span></span>

These interacting factors demonstrate that the violence was not simply the spontaneous product of frightened crowds. Rumours gained authority because respected institutions and influential voices repeated or failed to challenge them.

## Violence against Corfu's Jewish community

As the rumours spread, attacks on Jewish homes, businesses and neighbourhoods followed. Crowds assaulted Jewish residents, looted property and intimidated families. Military forces were eventually deployed, but their response was inconsistent and often failed to provide effective protection during the crucial early stages of the unrest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ejournals.epublishing.ekt.gr/index.php/historicalReview/article/view/3994" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ejournals.epublishing.ekt.gr">[eJournals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ejournals.epublishing.ekt.gr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Anti-Semitic Disturbances on Corfu and Zakynthos in 1891 and their Socio-political Consequences&#124;The Historical Review/La Rev...</span></span></span>

The violence continued for several weeks. Contemporary and later historical accounts differ slightly on the precise death toll, reflecting the difficulty of reconstructing events from conflicting records, but there is broad agreement that Jews were killed, many others were injured, and extensive material damage was inflicted on the community.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09518967.2012.730792" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tandfonline.com">[Taylor &amp; Francis Online]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tandfonline.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Taylor &amp; Francis Online Full article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Taylor &amp; Francis OnlineFull article: IntroductionMarch 14, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 14, 2013</span></span></span>

One of the most lasting consequences was demographic. Thousands of Corfu's Jews left the island in the aftermath, joining communities elsewhere in Greece, Italy, Egypt and beyond. Although Jewish life continued on Corfu, the events of 1891 marked a decisive weakening of a community that had existed on the island for many centuries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://blog.nli.org.il/en/corfu_jews/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blog.nli.org.il">[nli.org.il]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blog.nli.org.il</span><span class="citation-popover-title">הספרניםThe Lost History of the Jews of Corfu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">June 5, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 5, 2024</span></span></span>

## Why historians avoid calling the persecution "mass hysteria"

The Corfu events sometimes appear in discussions of collective belief because a demonstrably false accusation spread rapidly through a population. However, historians generally avoid describing the violence simply as "mass hysteria", since that label risks implying that irrational fear alone explains what happened.

Several features distinguish the episode from cases of mass psychogenic illness or crowd panic.


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* The central belief was an established antisemitic conspiracy theory rather than a novel delusion.
* The rumour spread through newspapers, officials and political actors as well as ordinary conversation.
* The violence targeted a specific minority that already faced discrimination.
* Judicial investigations produced evidence contradicting the accusation, yet many participants ignored those findings.

</div>

For these reasons, scholars increasingly interpret the Corfu blood libel as an episode of persecution shaped by rumour, prejudice and political incentives rather than as a psychological epidemic detached from social and institutional forces.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.quest-cdecjournal.it/antisemitic-rumours-and-violence-in-corfu-at-the-end-of-19th-century/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: quest-cdecjournal.it">[quest-cdecjournal.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">quest-cdecjournal.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Quest CDEC JournalAntisemitic Rumours and Violence in Corfu at the End of 19th Century - Quest. ## Why the Corfu blood libel still matters

The Corfu blood libel remains one of the clearest Greek examples of how conspiracy myths can produce real-world harm. The murder of Rubina Sardas was a genuine crime, but the victim herself became almost invisible as attention shifted towards an entirely invented story about ritual murder. That false narrative justified attacks on innocent people, accelerated the decline of one of Greece's oldest Jewish communities and attracted concern well beyond the island.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://blog.nli.org.il/en/corfu_jews/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blog.nli.org.il">[הספרנים]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blog.nli.org.il</span><span class="citation-popover-title">הספרניםThe Lost History of the Jews of Corfu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">June 5, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 5, 2024</span></span></span>

The episode also illustrates an important distinction for the wider history of collective fear. Not every rapidly spreading false belief should be understood as "mass hysteria". When rumours reinforce existing prejudice, are amplified by influential institutions and are directed against an identifiable minority, the more accurate framework is often one of moral panic, conspiracy thinking and organised persecution.
