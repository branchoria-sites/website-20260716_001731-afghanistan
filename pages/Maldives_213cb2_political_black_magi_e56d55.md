## Introduction

Belief in black magic has long existed in the [Maldives]({{ 'when-supernatural-fear-gripped-the/' | relative_url }}), but its appearance in modern politics is a much more specific phenomenon. Rather than reflecting a nationwide panic, recent episodes show how supernatural allegations can become political tools during periods of uncertainty, elite rivalry and intense public speculation. Rumours about sorcery have been used to explain unexpected political success, alleged shifts in influence around senior leaders, and conflicts within governing circles. The result is that claims which cannot be independently verified can nevertheless shape headlines, social media discussion and public perceptions of political events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hrw.org/news/2018/08/24/no-need-sorcery-stifle-democracy-maldives" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hrw.org">[Human Rights Watch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hrw.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Human Rights WatchNo Need for Sorcery to Stifle Democracy in the Maldives &#124; Human Rights WatchAugust 24, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 24, 2018</span></span></span>

The available evidence also requires caution. Some allegations have been repeated widely by media outlets and online commentators despite limited official confirmation. Distinguishing between documented police actions, political rumour and proven fact is therefore essential when examining the role of supernatural beliefs in Maldivian politics.

## Why black magic became a political explanation

The Maldives combines a modern electoral system with a society in which many people remain familiar with traditional beliefs about harmful supernatural practices. Although Islamic teaching condemns sorcery, belief that it can influence health, fortune or relationships has persisted in parts of society. This provides a cultural framework in which political events can sometimes be interpreted through supernatural rather than purely strategic or institutional explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://efe.com/en/latest-news/2024-06-26/maldives-police-arrests-minister-for-doing-black-magic-on-president-muizzu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: efe.com">[EFE Noticias]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">efe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Noticias Row Surrounding the Alleged Lack of Magic in the Maldives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">EFE NoticiasRow Surrounding the Alleged Lack of Magic in the Maldives - EFE Noticias...</span></span></span>

Political competition also creates fertile ground for invisible explanations. Elections often produce unexpected victories or defeats, shifting alliances and sudden changes in elite fortunes. Where political motives are difficult to observe, allegations of hidden supernatural influence can offer a dramatic narrative that appears to explain otherwise confusing events, even when no objective evidence exists.

## Sorcery claims around the 2018 election

During the period surrounding the 2018 presidential election, allegations involving black magic entered national political debate. The administration of President Abdulla Yameen was criticised after individuals were reportedly arrested over accusations of using black magic in support of opposition political figures. Human Rights Watch cited these arrests as part of a broader pattern in which state institutions were being used against political opponents before the election, arguing that such actions contributed to an increasingly repressive political climate rather than providing evidence of genuine supernatural offences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hrw.org/news/2018/08/24/no-need-sorcery-stifle-democracy-maldives" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hrw.org">[Human Rights Watch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hrw.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Human Rights WatchNo Need for Sorcery to Stifle Democracy in the Maldives &#124; Human Rights WatchAugust 24, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 24, 2018</span></span></span>

What is notable is not that sorcery accusations existed, but how they became intertwined with electoral politics. Rather than remaining private religious or community matters, they entered public political discourse alongside disputes over elections, opposition activity and state power. The accusations therefore functioned as political narratives as much as claims about supernatural forces.

There is little reliable evidence that these allegations affected voting behaviour directly. Their significance lies instead in illustrating how supernatural explanations could become part of political conflict during periods of democratic tension.

## The 2024 ministerial arrest and disputed reports

The most internationally reported example emerged in June 2024, when police arrested Environment State Minister Fathimath Shamnaz Ali together with two other individuals during an undisclosed criminal investigation. Multiple Maldivian and international news organisations reported widespread claims that the investigation concerned allegations of performing black magic against President Mohamed Muizzu.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.thepress.mv/16993" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.thepress.mv">[thepress.mv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.thepress.mv</span><span class="citation-popover-title">byline.mv State Minister arrested amid allegations of black magic &#124; The Press</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Minister arrested amid allegations of black magic &#124; ThePressJune 26, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 26, 2024</span></span></span>

The crucial distinction is that police confirmed the arrests but did not publicly confirm that black magic formed the basis of the criminal investigation. Local reporting consistently described the sorcery allegation as something circulating in media and political circles rather than an officially stated charge. Some outlets explicitly noted that the police declined to verify the reported motive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://atolltimes.mv/post/news/8876" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: atolltimes.mv">[atolltimes.mv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">atolltimes.mv</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Police arrest State Minister over &quot;black magic&quot; allegations &#124; Atoll Times</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">June 26, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 26, 2024</span></span></span>

This difference became blurred in international coverage. Headlines often stated that a minister had been arrested "for black magic", while the underlying articles frequently acknowledged that official confirmation was absent. As stories spread internationally, the distinction between an allegation reported by journalists and an established legal fact became less obvious.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.straitstimes.com/asia/maldives-climate-minister-arrested-over-black-magic" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: straitstimes.com">[straitstimes.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">straitstimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on straitstimes.com.</span></span></span>

The case also attracted attention because the minister had previously worked closely with President Muizzu in the Malé City Council before joining his administration, making speculation about political relationships and rivalries especially intense.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://efe.com/en/latest-news/2024-06-26/maldives-police-arrests-minister-for-doing-black-magic-on-president-muizzu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: efe.com">[EFE Noticias]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">efe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Noticias Row Surrounding the Alleged Lack of Magic in the Maldives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">EFE NoticiasRow Surrounding the Alleged Lack of Magic in the Maldives - EFE Noticias...</span></span></span>

## How headlines can turn allegations into apparent facts

The 2024 episode demonstrates how reporting on supernatural allegations can unintentionally create certainty where little exists.

Several patterns appeared repeatedly:

* **Police confirmed arrests**, but not necessarily the reported supernatural motive.
* **Media attributed the black magic claim to unnamed sources or other news reports**, rather than official charging documents.
* **International coverage simplified complex reporting**, often reducing uncertainty in headlines.
* **Social media amplified the most dramatic interpretation**, encouraging readers to treat allegations as established events rather than disputed claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.thepress.mv/16993" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.thepress.mv">[thepress.mv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.thepress.mv</span><span class="citation-popover-title">byline.mv State Minister arrested amid allegations of black magic &#124; The Press</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Minister arrested amid allegations of black magic &#124; ThePressJune 26, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 26, 2024</span></span></span>

This is a common feature of political rumour. Once a striking explanation gains attention, later reporting often repeats it with fewer qualifications, even if the underlying evidence remains limited.

## What these cases reveal about political belief

Neither the 2018 election-related accusations nor the 2024 ministerial investigation demonstrate that supernatural practices influenced political outcomes. Instead, they illustrate how belief in hidden forces can become part of political storytelling.

Several factors help explain why such allegations persist:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Invisible causes can appear persuasive when political events are unexpected.
* Religious beliefs about forbidden sorcery provide a culturally familiar framework.
* Political rivalry encourages rumours that damage opponents' reputations.
* Modern news cycles reward unusual stories that attract international attention.

</div>

For historians and social scientists, these episodes are therefore valuable less as evidence for supernatural intervention than as examples of how societies use culturally meaningful explanations during moments of political uncertainty.

## Why the allegations remain significant

Black magic allegations occupy an unusual place in contemporary Maldivian politics. They are neither simply remnants of folklore nor straightforward criminal cases. Instead, they reveal the interaction between traditional beliefs, religious ideas, modern media and political competition.

The most important lesson is methodological rather than supernatural. Readers should distinguish carefully between a documented arrest, an officially stated charge, a political rumour and a media interpretation. The 2024 case especially demonstrates how quickly those categories can merge in public discussion, creating an impression of certainty that exceeds the available evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://see.mv/90260" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: see.mv">[see.mv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">see.mv</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sun English Edition State Minister Shamnaz arrested &#124; Sun English Edition</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sun English Edition State Minister Shamnaz arrested &#124; Sun English Edition</span></span></span>
